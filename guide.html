COSTAR Spiral-Tuned Guitar: User Guide

The COSTAR Spiral-Tuned Guitar is a free web app that shows scales and modes on a guitar fretboard tuned in all fourths, and lights up the notes you play from a MIDI guitar. It runs in any Chrome-family web browser, with nothing to install.

Getting started

Open the app in a web browser. The first time, you see a six-string guitar neck showing C Ionian (the C major scale), with a bubble of keyboard shortcuts at the bottom.

  • The fretboard fills the screen. The nut is on the left, with the open-string names beside it and fret numbers underneath.
  • The badge at the top left names the key and mode, for example "C Ionian · root = white/black".
  • The MIDI status sits at the top right.
  • Settings open from the gear button at the top right, or with the H key. Every option in this guide lives there.
  • The shortcuts bubble opens and closes with K.
  • Full screen is F.

The app remembers your choices in that browser, so it opens the way you left it.

Keys and modes

Pick a key and a mode in Settings, or step through them with the arrow keys: ← → change the key a half step at a time, and ↑ ↓ step through all twelve modes.

Diatonic modes (chroma sequence): Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian and Locrian. Their seven notes appear as colored dots. Each color marks a scale degree of the mode's relative major scale:

Degree of the relative major 1 2 3 4 5 6 7
Dot color half white, half black green yellow orange red blue purple

In Ionian the root is the half white, half black dot. In other modes the root is another color: in Aeolian, for example, it is blue. The badge at the top left names the root's color, and the note under the mode buttons in Settings names the relative major.

Pentatonic modes (luma sequence): Ritsu, Minor, Major, Egyptian and Man Gong. Their five notes appear as black dots, and the badge names the root by its Greek letter (α, β, γ, δ or ε; see the next section).

Each mode button starts with its root's degree or Greek letter, for example 6-Aeolian or β-Minor.

Dots and labels

Two kinds of dots can be shown, each turned on or off in Settings > View.

  • Chroma sequence (C): the colored dots of a diatonic mode. They are only available in diatonic modes.
  • Luma sequence (L): black dots on the five notes a diatonic scale leaves out. In a pentatonic mode these five notes are the scale itself, so they turn on automatically.

Each black dot has a Greek letter, set by its distance above the relative major's 1: α = ♭2, β = ♭3, γ = ♭5, δ = ♭6 and ε = ♭7.

Labels on the dots:

  • Chroma dot labels (N): none, note names or scale degrees. Diatonic modes only.
  • Luma dot labels (G): none, Greek letters, note names (always flats) or scale degrees. Available in every mode.

Other dot options:

  • Dot size (S): 100%, 75% or 50%.
  • Dots (D): show all scale notes, or only the notes being played. "Only notes being played" leaves a clean board that lights up as you play.

Scale patterns

A scale pattern narrows the board to one playable position: only the pattern's dots stay, and any note you play outside it shows faintly.

  • P turns the pattern on and off. The app picks the kind that fits the mode: 3 notes per string in diatonic modes, 2 notes per string in pentatonic modes.
  • The pattern starts at a fret on the low string and climbs across the strings. Set that fret under Settings > Scale pattern > Starting fret.
  • [ and ] move the start to the previous or next scale note on the low string.
  • Shift-click a dot on the low string to start the pattern there.

Views, instrument and palettes

View (V):

  • Six strings shows a real guitar neck.
  • Twelve strings extends the same tuning to twelve rows a fourth apart, so the pattern of the scale repeats as a spiral. The six real strings sit in the middle, with lower rows above and higher rows below. Twelve strings can be drawn as a guitar neck or an abstract grid.

Instrument (Settings > Instrument):

  • Lowest open string: from A1 to B2. E2 gives E–A–D–G–C–F; D2 gives D–G–C–F–B♭–E♭.
  • Frets shown: 12 to 24.
  • Tuning: All Fourths (every string a fourth above the last) or Standard (a major third between the 4th and 5th strings from the bottom, like a regular guitar). Twelve strings works with All Fourths only.
  • Orientation (O): Standard puts the high string on top, as in guitar tab and most chord charts. Mirror puts the low string on top.

Hide info (I) hides the key badge, the MIDI status and the fret numbers and open-string names. Press M to peek at the MIDI status while the rest stays hidden.

Palettes (< >): nine color schemes, five light and four dark. > (the period key) steps forward, < (the comma key) steps back, and T also steps forward. The scale-degree colors keep their order in every palette; only their shades change.

Playing along with MIDI

Connect a MIDI guitar, guitar-to-MIDI converter or keyboard, and each note you play lights up at its position on the board.

Browsers: live MIDI works in Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge and other Chrome-family browsers on Mac, Windows and Android. It does not work in Safari or on any iPhone or iPad; there the board and click-to-play still work.

Connecting:

  1. Plug in your MIDI device before or after opening the app.
  2. When Chrome asks to allow MIDI devices, click Allow. If the status says MIDI is blocked, click the icon at the left of the address bar, allow MIDI devices, then reload.
  3. The MIDI status at the top right lists the inputs found. Settings > MIDI > Last note received confirms notes are arriving.

Settings > MIDI:

  • Input: All inputs, or one device.
  • String mapping: for a MIDI guitar sending one channel per string, choose channel 1 = high string or channel 1 = low string, so each note lights on the right string. For a keyboard, choose Pitch only: the note lights at every position where it can be played.
  • Transpose incoming notes: shifts notes up or down by up to 24 half steps, if your device sends them at the wrong pitch.
  • Show MIDI status on the fretboard (M).

No MIDI device? Click and hold any position on the board to light it.

Stuck notes: press Esc to clear any notes still lit.

Roland GR-20 tips: tune your guitar accurately, keep the patch's Transpose at 0, and set the MIDI mode to MONO (hold EXIT while switching the GR-20 on, choose Mono with the dial, press EXIT). Keep the mapping on "channel 1 = high string". To check, play your lowest open string: the dot left of the nut on the low string should light.

Song presets and Program Change

Song presets save a key and mode for each song, so you can switch between them with a click or a MIDI Program Change.

  • + Add preset (Settings > Song presets) adds a preset with the current key and mode. Give it a song name, and change its key or mode from its menus.
  • ▶ loads a preset; × removes it.
  • Each preset is numbered: PC 1, PC 2 and so on. An incoming MIDI Program Change 1 loads preset 1, Program Change 2 loads preset 2, and a notice at the top names what loaded.
  • Listen for Program Change on: pick the channel your controller or recording software sends on, so other gear (such as a guitar synth changing patches) doesn't load presets by accident.

Many programs number programs from 0 rather than 1. If program 1 in your software loads preset 2, send one lower.

Anything that sends Program Change can drive presets: a footswitch, a multi-effects unit, a keyboard, or recording software such as Logic Pro sending through the Mac's IAC Driver.

Keyboard shortcuts

Press K to show the shortcuts bubble on screen. Shortcuts pause while you type in a number box or menu in Settings; click the fretboard to resume.

Saved settings, sharing a setup, and privacy

Saved settings: your choices and song presets are kept in the browser you use, on that device. A different browser, a different device or a private window starts fresh, with the defaults.

Sharing a setup in a link: add options to the end of the app's web address, after a ?, joined with &. Anyone opening the link sees the board set that way. For example, ?key=7&mode=dorian&theme=neon opens G Dorian in the Neon palette.

Privacy: site visits are counted anonymously, without cookies. The app collects nothing you type, and your settings stay in your browser.
