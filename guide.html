COSTAR Spiral-Tuned Guitar: User Guide

The COSTAR Spiral-Tuned Guitar is a free web app that displays scales and modes on a guitar fretboard tuned in all fourths, highlighting the notes you play from a MIDI guitar.

The note color sequence is that of the COSTAR system and its chromatic lunar-solar calendar.

COSTAR: Cycles Of Song, Theme, And Ritual

The web app runs in any Chrome-family web browser, with nothing to install.

Getting started

Open the app in a web browser by going to nfrhtp.github.io/spiral/. The first time, you see a six-string guitar neck showing C Ionian (the C major scale), with a bubble of keyboard shortcuts at the bottom.

  • The fretboard fills the screen. The nut is on the left, with the open-string note names beside it and some fret numbers underneath.
  • The badge at the top left of the screen names the key and mode, and identifies the color of the root note dots. For example: "C Ionian · root = white/black".
  • The MIDI status sits at the top right.
  • Settings open from the gear button at the top right, or with the H key. Every option in this guide can be set there.
  • The shortcuts bubble opens and closes with K.
  • Full screen is F.

The app remembers your choices in that browser, so it opens the way you left it.

Keys and modes

Pick a key and a mode in Settings, or step through them with the arrow keys: ← → change the key a half step at a time, and ↑ ↓ step through all twelve modes.

Diatonic modes (chroma sequence): Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian and Locrian. Their seven notes appear as colored dots. Each color marks a scale degree of the mode's relative major scale:

Degree of the relative major Chroma sequence Root of which mode?
1 white/black Ionian
2 green Dorian
3 yellow Phrygian
4 orange Lydian
5 red Mixolydian
6 blue Aeolian
7 purple Locrian

In Ionian the root is the half white, half black dot. In other modes the root is another color: in Aeolian, for example, it is blue. The badge at the top left names the root's color, and in Settings, the note under the mode buttons names the relative major.

Pentatonic modes (luma sequence): Ritsu, Minor, Major, Egyptian and Man Gong. Their five notes appear as black dots.

Luma sequence Pentatonic scale
α Ritsu
β Minor
γ Major
δ Egyptian
ε Man Gong

Dots and labels

Two kinds of dots can be shown, each turned on or off in Settings > View.

  • Chroma sequence (C): the colored dots of a diatonic mode. They are only available in diatonic modes.
  • Luma sequence (L): black dots on the five notes a diatonic scale leaves out. In a pentatonic mode these five notes are the scale itself, so they turn on automatically.

Each black dot has a Greek letter, set by its distance above the relative major's 1: α = ♭2, β = ♭3, γ = ♭5, δ = ♭6 and ε = ♭7.

Labels on the dots:

  • Chroma dot labels (N): none, note names or scale degrees. Diatonic modes only.
  • Luma dot labels (G): none, Greek letters, note names (always flats) or scale degrees. Available in every mode.

Other dot options:

  • Dot size (S): 100%, 75% or 50%.
  • Dots (D): show all scale notes, or only the notes being played. "Only notes being played" leaves a clean board that lights up as you play.

Scale patterns

A scale pattern narrows the display of dots to one playable position: only the pattern's dots stay, and any note you play outside it shows faintly.

  • P turns the pattern on and off. The app picks the kind that fits the mode: 3 notes per string in diatonic modes, 2 notes per string in pentatonic modes.
  • The pattern starts at a fret on the low string and climbs across the strings. Set that fret under Settings > Scale pattern > Starting fret.
  • [ and ] move the start to the previous or next scale note on the low string.
  • Shift-click a dot on the low string to start the pattern there.

Views, instrument and palettes

View (V):

  • Six strings depicts a real guitar neck.
  • Twelve strings extends the same tuning to twelve rows a fourth apart, so the pattern of the scale repeats as a spiral. The six real strings sit in the middle, with lower rows above and higher rows below. Twelve strings can be drawn as a guitar neck or an abstract grid. Twelve strings works with All Fourths only.

Instrument (Settings > Instrument):

  • Lowest open string: from A1 to B2. E2 gives E–A–D–G–C–F; D2 gives D–G–C–F–B♭–E♭.
  • Frets shown: 12 to 24.
  • Tuning: All Fourths (every string a fourth above the last) or Standard (a major third between the 4th and 5th strings from the bottom, like a regular guitar). This app is focussed on explorations of All Fourths tuning, but can be helpful with Standard tuning as well.
  • Orientation (O): Standard puts the high string on top, as in guitar tab and most chord charts. Mirror puts the low string on top.

Hide info (I) hides from the screen the key badge, the MIDI status, the fret numbers, and the open-string names. Press M to peek at the MIDI status while the rest stays hidden.

Palettes (< >): nine color schemes, five light and four dark. > (the period key) steps forward, < (the comma key) steps back. The scale-degree colors keep their order in every palette; only their shades change.

Playing along with MIDI

Connect a MIDI guitar, guitar-to-MIDI converter or keyboard, and each note you play lights up at its position on the board.

Browsers: live MIDI works in Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge and other Chrome-family browsers on Mac, Windows and Android. It does not work in Safari or on any iPhone or iPad; there the board and click-to-play still work.

Connecting:

  1. Plug in your MIDI device before or after opening the app.
  2. When Chrome asks to allow MIDI devices, click Allow. If the status says MIDI is blocked, click the icon at the left of the address bar, allow MIDI devices, then reload.
  3. The MIDI status at the top right lists the inputs found. In the app's Settings panel, watch MIDI > Last note received to confirm that notes are arriving.

Settings > MIDI:

  • Input: All inputs, or one device.
  • String mapping: for a MIDI guitar sending one channel per string, choose channel 1 = high string or channel 1 = low string, so each note lights on the right string. For a keyboard, choose Pitch only: the note lights at every position where it can be played.
  • Transpose incoming notes: shifts notes up or down by up to 24 half steps. Helpful if your device sends them at the wrong pitch.
  • Show MIDI status on the fretboard (M).

No MIDI device? Click and hold any position on the board to light it.

Stuck notes: press Esc to clear any notes still lit.

Roland GR-20 tips: tune your guitar accurately, keep the patch's Transpose at 0, and set the MIDI mode to MONO (hold EXIT while switching the GR-20 on, choose Mono with the dial, press EXIT). Keep the mapping on "channel 1 = high string". To check, play your lowest open string: the dot left of the nut on the low string should light.

Song presets and Program Change

Song presets save a key and mode for each song, so you can switch between them with a click or a MIDI Program Change.

  • + Add preset (Settings > Song presets) adds a preset with the current key and mode. Give it a song name, and change its key or mode from its menus.
  • ▶ loads a preset; × deletes it.
  • Each preset is numbered: PC 1, PC 2 and so on. An incoming MIDI Program Change 1 loads preset 1, Program Change 2 loads preset 2, and a notice at the top of the fretboard screen names what loaded.
  • Listen for Program Change on: pick the channel your controller or recording software sends on, so other gear (such as a guitar synth changing patches) doesn't load presets by accident.

Many programs number programs from 0 rather than 1. If program 1 in your software loads preset 2, send one lower.

Anything that sends Program Change can drive presets: a footswitch, a multi-effects unit, a keyboard, or recording software such as Logic Pro sending through the Mac's IAC Driver.

Example: Program Change from Logic Pro on a Mac

This example shows one setup that works: Logic Pro on a Mac, with the app open in Google Chrome. Other recording software and computers need similar steps: a virtual MIDI connection from the software to the browser, and a track that sends Program Change through it.

The example sends on channel 16. That isn't required, but a channel used only for song presets keeps Program Change messages from other gear (such as a guitar synth changing patches) from loading presets by accident.

Numbering: Logic counts programs from 0 and the app counts presets from 1. Logic program 0 loads preset 1, program 1 loads preset 2, and so on.

1. Turn on the Mac's IAC Driver

The IAC Driver is a virtual MIDI cable built into macOS.

  1. Open the Audio MIDI Setup app (in Applications > Utilities).
  2. Choose Window > Show MIDI Studio.
  3. Double-click IAC Driver.
  4. Tick Device is online and close the window.

If Chrome or Logic was open when you did this, quit it completely (Cmd+Q) and reopen it so it sees the new connection.

2. Set up the app

In the app's Settings:

  • MIDI > Input: All inputs (or IAC Driver Bus 1).
  • Song presets > Listen for Program Change on: Channel 16.
  • Song presets: make one preset for each key and mode you want to call up.

3. Make a Logic track that sends to the IAC Driver

  1. Create an External MIDI track.
  2. Select the track and press I to show the Inspector on the left.
  3. Open the Track section by clicking its disclosure triangle.
  4. Set MIDI Output to IAC Driver Bus 1 (it starts as "Off").
  5. Set MIDI Out Channel to 16.

The Rev and Chor knobs in this track's mixer strip play no part here.

4. Test it

  1. In the track's Inspector, tick the checkbox to the left of Program. With the box unticked, changing the number sends nothing.
  2. Pick a program number. Logic sends it right away.
  3. A notice at the top of the fretboard screen shows "PC 1: …", or "no preset" if that number has no preset yet. Either one means the connection works.

5. Put the changes into a song

  1. On the track, Control-click at bar 1 and choose Create MIDI Region. Drag its right edge so it covers the song.
  2. Select the region and open the Event List: press D and click the Event tab, or choose Window > Open Event List. (In Logic Pro 12, E opens the Piano Roll, not the Event List.)
  3. Move the playhead to where the change should happen.
  4. Set the event-type menu next to + to Program Change and click +.
  5. Set the event's position, channel 16, and the program number.
  6. Repeat for each change, then press Play. The fretboard switches as the playhead passes each event.

Starting mid-song: turn on File > Project Settings > MIDI > Chase > Program Change, so Logic sends the latest Program Change whenever you start or stop playback.

After setup: you can untick the Inspector's Program box. Otherwise it sends its own number whenever you select the track or open the project.

If nothing arrives

  • Is the Program box in the Inspector ticked (for the test)?
  • Does MIDI Output still say IAC Driver Bus 1, not Off?
  • In the app's Settings > MIDI, does the Status line list IAC Driver Bus 1? If not, quit and reopen Chrome.
  • Do Logic's MIDI Out Channel and the app's Listen for Program Change on match (both 16, or the app set to Any channel)?

Saved settings, sharing a setup, and privacy

Saved settings: your choices and song presets are kept in the browser you use, on that device. A different browser, a different device or a private window starts fresh, with the defaults.

Sharing a setup in a link: add options to the end of the app's web address, after a ?, joined with &. Anyone opening the link sees the board set that way. For example, ?key=7&mode=dorian&theme=neon opens G Dorian in the Neon palette.
